Kampala's commuter rail opens Monday: routes, fares and what changes for your commute

Four stations, two peak-hour loops and a flat introductory fare — everything we know about the city's biggest transport shift in a decade.

G
GumkomaJul 12, 2026 · 6 min read · Kampala

After years of announcements, delays and one very public test run that stopped halfway, Kampala's commuter rail service finally opens to passengers on Monday morning. For a city where the average commute has been getting longer every year, it is the most significant change to how people move since the boda boom.

Here is what is confirmed so far, what is still unclear, and what it means for your daily journey.

The routes

The service launches with two peak-hour loops running in both directions — an eastern line and a southern line — connecting four stations to the central platform downtown. Trains are scheduled every 30 minutes between 6am and 9am, and again between 4:30pm and 8pm. Off-peak service is promised "within the first year" but has no date attached.

The fares

Pricing is the headline: a flat introductory fare for any journey, regardless of distance, for the first three months. Payment is cash at the station or mobile money at the gate. Officials say the flat fare is deliberately set below the equivalent taxi stage fare on the same corridors — the plan is to build the habit first and adjust prices later.

What it means for the road

Transport planners estimate each full train takes the equivalent of roughly 15 taxis' worth of passengers off the road per trip. Even at launch capacity, that is a measurable dent in peak congestion on the two corridors — though anyone expecting empty roads by Tuesday will be disappointed. The bigger effect, analysts say, comes if the service proves reliable enough to change where people choose to live and work.

"The first month is not about moving the most people. It's about proving the train leaves on time." — A transport official involved in the launch, speaking to Kumalo

What's still unclear

Three questions remain open going into launch week:

  • Feeder transport. The stations are useful only if you can reach them; talks with taxi and boda associations about staging areas were still ongoing as of Friday.
  • Capacity at peak. The morning eastern loop is expected to be oversubscribed from day one. There is no standing-room policy published yet.
  • The fare after month three. No pricing schedule beyond the introductory period has been released.

We will be riding the first eastern service on Monday morning and updating this story with what launch day actually looks like — queues, timings and all.
